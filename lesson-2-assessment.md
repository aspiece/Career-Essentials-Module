# Lesson 2 Assessment: Workplace Communication — The 7 Cs

**Total: 20 points**  
**Format: 10 questions worth 2 points each**

## Student assessment

Begin with the final workplace message. Then choose the best answer for Questions 1–9.

### Final workplace message (2 points)

Using the career field you selected in Step 2.4, enter one polished workplace message. Your message must name the audience, state a specific task, include actionable details that tell the receiver what to do and a deadline, request a next step or confirmation, and use a professional tone.

### 1. What is the most likely result of an unclear workplace message? (2 points)

A. The receiver asks for details.  
B. The receiver finishes early.  
C. The receiver skips all work.  
D. The receiver changes roles.

### 2. Which opening names the audience most clearly? (2 points)

A. Hi teammate, please restock.  
B. Hi someone, please restock.

D. Hi worker, please restock.

### 3. Which message is most concrete? (2 points)

A. Restock supplies today.  
B. Restock supplies later.

D. Restock supplies soon.

### 4. A message says, “Please inspect the safety kit.” What addition makes it more complete? (2 points)

### 5. Which message uses a courteous tone? (2 points)

### 7. Which detail should a writer check for correctness before sending a message? (2 points)

### 8. Which sequence is most coherent for a workplace message? (2 points)

## Instructor answer key and scoring guidance

| Question | Correct answer | Points | Rationale |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | A | 2 | An unclear message commonly requires the receiver to ask for needed details. |
| 2 | C | 2 | A specific name clearly identifies the receiver. |
| 3 | C | 2 | It names a specific item and deadline. |
| 4 | D | 2 | A deadline and requested reply help the receiver act and confirm completion. |
| 5 | C | 2 | “Please” gives a respectful, professional direction. |
| 6 | D | 2 | It uses few words while naming the item, location, and specific deadline. |
| 7 | A | 2 | Incorrect timing or location can prevent the receiver from completing work correctly. |
| 8 | D | 2 | The task comes first, followed by details, a deadline, and the requested response. |
| 9 | A | 2 | It identifies the exact equipment and deadline. |

### Final workplace message scoring (2 points)

Score the final message as a whole. Award full credit when it communicates the work clearly enough for the receiver to act without needing to guess.

| Score | Instructor guidance |
|---:|---|
| 2 | The message meets all four criteria: it names an appropriate audience; gives a specific task; includes actionable details (details that tell the receiver what to do), a deadline, and a requested next step or confirmation; and uses a clear, organized, courteous tone. |
| 1 | The message meets two or three criteria, or it has a minor omission that would require limited follow-up. Examples include a missing deadline, unclear audience, or no request for confirmation. |
| 0 | The message is incomplete, too vague to act on, or does not use a professional tone. It meets zero or one criterion. |

Use the same score for messages from every career field. Evaluate the quality of the communication rather than specialized technical knowledge.
