LESSON OVERVIEW

Workplace Communication: The 7 Cs

Clear communication helps people complete work safely, accurately, and on time. In this lesson, you will use the 7 Cs to improve a workplace message so the receiver understands what to do next.

Learning objectives

  1. Identify the 7 Cs in workplace messages.
  2. Explain how an unclear message can affect work.
  3. Revise a workplace message to make it clear, complete, and courteous.

National career-readiness alignment

  • Advance CTE Career Ready Practice: Communicate clearly, effectively, and within reason.
  • NACE Communication Competency: exchange information clearly and effectively inside and outside an organization.

STEP 2.4 · PRACTICAL APPLICATION

Revise a New Workplace Request

Estimated time: 18 minutes

Your next move: Use a new work order in your selected career field to write a message someone can act on. This task is different from the worked example.

Each career field has its own notes and draft. Switching fields keeps the work you already wrote; switch back to reopen it. You can use a career-program task without having a job.

Choose a career field to receive a new work order.

Use the supplied facts. These are fictional communication tasks. Follow your program's required supervision for any real work. If a real instruction is missing a fact, ask the sender; do not invent a deadline, location, or technical step.

1. Plan the recipient and task

Use the person or role in the work order. Choose a greeting that fits that audience.

Use an action verb and identify the item, place, or result from the work order.

2. Write your complete message

Optional frame: Hi [recipient], please [task]. Include [needed facts]. Finish by [deadline], then [confirmation]. Thank you.

Keep the facts accurate. Add the details, deadline, and requested reply in a logical order. This editor and Step 2.5 show the same response.

3. Find evidence in your draft

Read your draft. Check each statement only when you can point to the words that support it. This is self-review, not automatic grading.

4. Explain one improvement

Choose one C you improved. Use a phrase from your own draft to explain how it helps the receiver. One phrase may demonstrate more than one C.

For example, a specific time helps the receiver plan when to finish. Explain the effect of the words you chose.

5. Compare, then revise

After you have a draft, compare it with one possible response. Different wording can meet the same criteria. Revise your editor using what you notice.
