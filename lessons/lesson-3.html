LESSON OVERVIEW

Trust & Goal Setting at Work

At the Rock Wall, people depend on one another to follow through. At work, trust grows the same way: people communicate clearly, complete their responsibilities, and take ownership when something changes.

Learning objectives

  1. Identify behaviors that build or weaken workplace trust.
  2. Explain how accountability supports a team.
  3. Create a SMART goal for a workplace skill or task.

National career-readiness alignment

  • Advance CTE Career Ready Practice: act as a responsible and contributing citizen and employee.
  • NACE Professionalism Competency: demonstrate personal accountability and effective work habits.

Turn Intentions into SMART Goals

Estimated time: 11 minutes

Before you compare the goals: Click each part of SMART to review its meaning and workplace example.

Watch: How to Set SMART Goals

As you watch, listen for the five parts that turn a general intention into a usable goal.

Open the SMART-goals video in a new tab

Video transcript-based text alternative: How to Set SMART Goals

SMART goals make an objective easier to act on and review. A goal is specific when it names the exact action or result. It is measurable when you can show evidence of progress. It is achievable when it is realistic with available time and support. It is relevant when it matters to the work, team, or career goal. It is time-bound when it includes a deadline or check-in date.
