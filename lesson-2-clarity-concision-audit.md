# Lesson 2 Clarity and Concision Audit

**Audience reviewed:** Grade 11 students completing the lesson independently  
**Scope:** Student-facing lesson page, including vocabulary popups, checks, career-field comparison, application, and assessment directions.

## Overall finding

The lesson is clear, workplace-relevant, and appropriate for 11th-grade students. Sentences are generally short, directions use direct verbs, and every abstract communication term is supported by a definition, workplace purpose, and example. The career-field Before/After comparison is a particularly strong bridge from concept to application.

The lesson can become more concise and less ambiguous with six targeted revisions. None require changing the lesson objective, assessment, or activity sequence.

## What is working well

| Area | Evidence | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | The opening explains that clear communication supports safe, accurate, on-time work. | Students understand why the lesson matters before beginning. |
| Vocabulary | Terms have a definition, workplace context, example, and repeatable popup links. | The support is appropriate for a mixed-readiness Grade 11 group without reducing rigor. |
| Directions | Each step includes an estimated time and a “Your next move” prompt. | The independent-work flow is easy to follow. |
| Career relevance | Students select a field and view a matched Before/After message. | The model is concrete and makes the 7 Cs visible in an authentic context. |
| Practice | Checks move from identifying a problem to applying the framework. | Cognitive demand builds toward the final workplace message. |

## Recommended revisions

| Priority | Location | Finding | Recommended revision |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | Step 2.4 field labels | “Your improved audience” and “Your improved task” can imply that the audience or task itself changes. | Use **“1. Name the audience”** and **“2. State the task.”** |
| High | Step 2.5 final-response direction | It says to use the field selected in Step 2.4, but students select the field in Step 2.3. | Change **“Use the career field you selected in Step 2.4”** to **“Use the career field you selected in Step 2.3.”** |
| High | Step 2.4 four-Cs activity | “Specific” and “respectful” are student-friendly descriptions, but students must select the formal terms **concrete** and **courteous**. | State the mapping: **“Choose clear, concrete (specific), complete, and courteous (respectful).”** |
| Medium | Step 2.3 comparison heading | “Watch a message improve” suggests another video even though students read two messages. | Change to **“Compare a message before and after.”** |
| Medium | Step 2.4 revised-message prompt | “Needed supplies” does not fit every career field; for example, a website or social-media task may require information rather than supplies. | Replace **“needed supplies”** with **“needed details.”** |
| Medium | Step 2.2 reflection | Students are asked to think about confusing instructions, but they are not told whether to write, discuss, or simply think. | Add **“Think privately; you do not need to write a response.”** |

## Concision opportunities

- Keep the seven-Cs reading alternative because it is an important access support. It is longer than the surrounding text, so retaining it inside the closed transcript panel is the right choice.
- The standards section is accurate but does not directly support the student task. Consider placing it after the pacing table, labeling it **“Why this matters for your career,”** or making it an optional details panel.
- The phrase “Your next move” is useful as a consistent cue. Continue using it once per step, as the lesson currently does.
- The current examples are concise enough for an 11th-grade audience. Do not shorten the improved messages further; their details model the communication behaviors students must use.

## Reading-level judgment

The main lesson directions and questions use familiar words, active verbs, and short sentence patterns. The specialized terms are introduced with accessible definitions and linked examples. This places the student-facing work below typical Grade 11 reading demand while preserving appropriate career-readiness rigor. The only denser language appears in the standards links and the optional transcript directions.

## Alignment check

The sequence remains aligned:

1. Students learn the 7 Cs.
2. Students identify communication problems in short messages.
3. Students compare a field-specific vague message with an improved model.
4. Students plan and revise their own message.
5. Students submit a final workplace message for scoring.

## Decision

**Status: Ready for the six targeted clarity revisions above.** The lesson does not need structural changes for an 11th-grade audience.
